  <article class="course">
    <div class="course-term">SS 2026</div>
    <div>
      <h2>Algebraic Complexity Theory</h2>
      <p class="course-meta">Universität Regensburg · Co-instructor with {% include person-link.html key="bhargav" name="Bhargav CS" %} and {% include person-link.html key="shukla" name="Himanshu Shukla" %}</p>
      <div class="course-actions">
        <details class="course-description">
          <summary>Description</summary>
          <div class="course-description-body">
            <p>
              A graduate introduction to algebraic and geometric complexity theory, following the first
              twelve chapters of Bläser and Ikenmeyer’s
              <a href="https://theoryofcomputing.org/articles/gs010/gs010.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><em>Introduction to Geometric Complexity Theory</em></a>. The goal is to develop the algebraic,
              geometric, and representation-theoretic tools needed to study the permanent-versus-determinant
              problem and the border rank of matrix multiplication.
            </p>
            <p><strong>Chapters covered:</strong></p>
            <ul>
              <li>Boolean circuits and arithmetic circuits</li>
              <li>Waring rank and border Waring rank</li>
              <li>Actions, orbits, and orbit closures</li>
              <li>First algebraic geometry</li>
              <li>Algebraic complexity classes: \(\mathsf{VP}\), \(\mathsf{VNP}\), and orbit problems</li>
              <li>\(\mathsf{VP}\) and the determinant</li>
              <li>The permanent and \(\mathsf{VP} \neq \mathsf{VNP}\)</li>
              <li>Border complexity and group orbit closures</li>
              <li>Representation theory</li>
              <li>Representation theory of finite groups and Maschke’s theorem</li>
              <li>Irreducible representations of \(\mathrm{GL}_n\): first properties</li>
              <li>Schur’s lemma, multiplicities, and isotypic decompositions</li>
            </ul>
          </div>
        </details>
        <details class="course-duties">
          <summary>Responsibilities</summary>
          <ul>
            <li>Taught the first four chapters: Boolean and arithmetic circuits, Waring rank, orbit closures, and first algebraic geometry.</li>
            <li>Designed and graded the exercise sheets.</li>
            <li>Conducted and graded the oral exam.</li>
          </ul>
        </details>
      </div>
    </div>

  <article class="course">
    <div class="course-term">10/2025–11/2025</div>
    <div>
      <h2>Computational Complexity</h2>
      <p class="course-meta">University of Warsaw · Teaching assistant · Main instructor: {% include person-link.html key="pilipczuk" name="Marcin Pilipczuk" %}</p>
      <div class="course-actions">
        <details class="course-description">
          <summary>Description</summary>
          <div class="course-description-body">
            <p>
              A rigorous introduction to computational complexity, from models of computation and basic
              complexity classes to reductions, NP-completeness, randomized computation, interactive
              proofs, cryptography, and fine-grained complexity.
            </p>
            <p><strong>Topics:</strong></p>
            <ul>
              <li>Decision and function problems; Turing machines, RAM, and Boolean circuits.</li>
              <li>Time and space complexity; the main complexity classes: \(\mathsf{NC}\), \(\mathsf{L}\), \(\mathsf{NL}\), \(\mathsf{P}\), \(\mathsf{NP}\), \(\mathsf{PSPACE}\), \(\mathsf{P/poly}\), and \(\mathsf{BPP}\).</li>
              <li>Polynomial-time computation, the \(\mathsf{P} \neq \mathsf{NP}\) conjecture, reductions, and NP-complete problems.</li>
              <li>Approximation algorithms, fixed-parameter tractability, and average polynomial complexity.</li>
              <li>Randomized computation, probabilistic classes, pseudorandom generators, and derandomization.</li>
              <li>\(\mathsf{PSPACE}\), alternating machines, interactive proofs, and games against Nature.</li>
              <li>One-way functions, cryptography, and zero-knowledge proofs.</li>
              <li>Diagonalization and hierarchy theorems; limits of diagonalization for \(\mathsf{P} \neq \mathsf{NP}\).</li>
              <li>Fine-grained and parameterized complexity; the Exponential Time Hypothesis.</li>
            </ul>
          </div>
        </details>
        <details class="course-duties">
          <summary>Responsibilities</summary>
          <ul>
            <li>Teaching assistant for the course.</li>
            <li>Gave a substitute lecture on the Immerman–Szelepcsényi theorem.</li>
            <li>Prepared NP-hardness exercise sheets and graded them.</li>
            <li>Solved exercise sheets for students, explained the solutions, and clarified doubts.</li>
          </ul>
        </details>
      </div>
    </div>
  </article>

    <div>
      <h2>Complexity Theory of Polynomial-Time Problems</h2>
      <p class="course-meta">Saarland University · Teaching assistant · Main instructors: {% include person-link.html key="bringmann" name="Karl Bringmann" %}, {% include person-link.html key="krinninger" name="Sebastian Krinninger" %}</p>
      <div class="course-actions">
        <details class="course-description">
          <summary>Description</summary>
          <div class="course-description-body">
            <p>
              An advanced course (2+1, 5 credits) on fine-grained complexity: a more precise view of
              the complexity of polynomial-time problems, with matching upper and lower bounds and
              connections between seemingly unrelated problems.
            </p>
            <p><strong>Topics:</strong></p>
            <ul>
              <li>Conditional lower bounds from \(\mathsf{SETH}\) and Orthogonal Vectors.</li>
              <li>The polynomial method for Orthogonal Vectors and All-Pairs Shortest Paths.</li>
              <li>Subcubic equivalences between path, matrix, and triangle problems.</li>
              <li>\(\mathsf{3SUM}\) algorithms and hardness.</li>
              <li>Boolean matrix multiplication: upper bounds and hardness.</li>
              <li>Dynamic graph algorithms: upper and lower bounds.</li>
              <li>Nondeterministic \(\mathsf{SETH}\) and the \(\mathsf{OMv}\) conjecture.</li>
            </ul>
            <p>
              The course includes six exercise sheets; students design conditional lower bounds and
              must collect at least 50% of the points to be admitted to the oral exam.
            </p>
          </div>
        </details>
        <details class="course-duties">
          <summary>Responsibilities</summary>
          <ul>
            <li>Held exercise sessions.</li>
            <li>Clarified students’ doubts.</li>
